# PRODUCT.md — Sozoris

## Register
Brand / marketing site. Landing-page-shaped, brand-forward, conversion-oriented. Not an app.

## Product purpose
Marketing site for **Sozoris**, a security firm (formerly **SubZeroSec**). It explains
five services (Penetration Testing/VAPT, Red Teaming, Threat Hunting, OSINT & Exposure,
Attack Surface Analysis), builds credibility through **verifiable disclosed research**
and a defence-minded process, and converts visitors into **booked security assessments**.

Name: **Sozoris**, from the Greek σῴζω (*sōzō*) — to save, to preserve, to make whole.
Tagline: **"Break it. Prove it. Make it whole."** — offensive testing is the method;
a proven, retested fix is the promise. The engagement ends when it holds, not when the
PDF ships.

## Positioning shift from SubZeroSec
Same services, same people, reframed. SubZeroSec was offense-first ("find the zero-day
before they do", "join the offense"). Sozoris keeps offense as the **method** and makes
**preservation the outcome**: we break what you built, prove exactly how, and stay until
every finding is closed and retested. Copy leads with what is still standing the day we
leave, not with how sharp the attack was.

## Target users
- **Security leaders** (CISO, Director of Security Operations) evaluating a partner.
- **Technical evaluators** (security engineers) who judge depth and rigor, not marketing.
- **Procurement / compliance** confirming how findings map to their own audits.

Primary conversion: "Book an assessment" / "Book a free consultation".

## Proof stance (important)
This is a young, independent firm, and the site says so. We do **not** claim scheme
accreditations we don't hold (no CREST/CHECK/QSA badges), invented head-count stats, or
un-checkable client testimonials — all of which the previous site carried. Instead the
proof is **externally verifiable disclosed research** (Better Auth, n8n `CVE-2026-33720`,
SAP, Twilio, FusionAuth), surfaced as a named **disclosure ledger** on the home, company
and trust pages. The trust page states plainly where the firm stands on formal
accreditation. Anonymized case studies remain as **illustrative structure** — swap them
for real, named-with-permission engagements before launch.

## Brand personality
Precise, technical, confident, defence-minded. Proof over promises. Calm authority, not
fear-mongering. Reads like operators who compromise systems for a living — because they
do — but who measure themselves on what holds afterwards.

## Anti-references (what to avoid)
- Generic SaaS sites with purple/blue AI gradients and three identical feature cards.
  (The old navy palette leaned into this; the new palettes deliberately break away.)
- Fear-mongering security vendors (red alarms, padlock clichés, breach-countdown theatrics).
- Padlock and shield marks — too literal; the Sozoris mark is a sealed open ring instead.
- "Scanner reseller" energy — automated-tool dashboards presented as expertise.
- Un-verifiable proof — badge walls, round-number stats and anonymous quotes.

## Strategic design principles
1. **Proof you can check.** Lead with evidence a third party has confirmed, not claims.
2. **Technical restraint.** Warm, sparse, legible. One accent + one metal, no decoration
   for its own sake.
3. **Method vs promise.** Show the adversary's-eye view, then the whole you're left with.
4. **Legibility first.** Dense security content stays scannable; numerics in mono metal.
